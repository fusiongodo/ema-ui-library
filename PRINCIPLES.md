# Design principles

The yardstick for every screen. If a design breaks one of these, it's wrong, however good it looks.

1. **Effort is the scarcest resource.** Every screen answers: what is the least the user has to do here?
2. **Neutral = as planned.** Defaults are invisible. Only deviations get marks or color.
3. **Show only what isn't default.** "Daily" and "1 entry" are not printed.
4. **Configuration lives where it is used.** Adding or changing opens a sheet over the current view, never a separate settings hop.
5. **Voice is the shortcut, taps the fallback.** The AI only writes drafts; nothing is saved without confirmation.
6. **History is versioned, never overwritten.** Edits apply from today; past days keep their version.
7. **Calm over busy.** One primary action per screen. Readable text never shrinks.
8. **Color is a signal, not decoration.** Tiles are white. At most one tinted tile per screen: the one thing that wants something from the user right now ("Check in now", "Set up your protocols"). Deviations get a mark, not a fill.
9. **Color = place.** Every area has exactly one color. Everything that belongs to an area or leads there wears it, on whatever screen it sits: "Check in now" is the Check-ins color on Today too. A sheet wears the color of its own area, not of the screen below.
10. **Few words, proper terms.** Reading costs energy. An explanation is a picture plus at most two short sentences (about 20 to 25 words). The tone is relaxed everyday language that still uses the proper term ("placebo", "blinded", "washout period"); the term is explained on demand behind an info icon, not paraphrased on the screen.
11. **Today is the reception.** Everything else is the back office. Today says what a good personal assistant would tell you: only what you need or profit from most, one quiet dismissible line at a time, and nothing when nothing is due. Health data is shown against "your usual", never against a goal.
