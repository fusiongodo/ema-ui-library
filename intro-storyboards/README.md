# Intro: where we stand

Quick access to everything about the intro animation for MEjority: what Alex liked, what he rejected, what is open. Updated 19 Nov 2026.

## Current direction: "The cloud"

On 19 Nov 2026 Alex described his own picture: one faint star, pulling back to many, the flat picture gains depth and turns out to be a rotating cloud around one centre, a few stars send something in, the centre answers all at the same instant, the charged stars jump to a line at the top, fall like rain, two bell curves grow from the ground, the points become a turning circle, hands, "MEjority". The full sequence is in **`script-cloud.md`**. In the evening he tightened it: **5 to 7 seconds in all**, a turning sphere, at the end a swirl of three points that hands cover and reveal as the wordmark (section "Short version" there). That short version is the one to build. It builds on what he liked below.

## What Alex likes, in his order

1. **First sketch "One dot each"** → `sketches/one-dot-each.png`. His favourite so far. One dot, then stars everywhere, then the lines to a centre, then the curve (real rising above placebo), then the burst of light back to all, then day. "Die Geschichte richtig geil."
2. **Second sketch** → `sketches/one-dot-each-2.png`. He finds the concepts cool (a star is a person and stays; a spark is a data point and travels; the cloud sorts into real and placebo). **Frame 5 with the solid disc is bad** ("scheiße"); the burst in the first sketch's frame 5 is the one to keep.
3. **Motion study 6, "Verteilung"** → `motion/index.html`. Points that pile up into two bell curves: "Punkte und ein statistisches Konzept. Finde ich nice."

Also liked: showing the whole process of statistics and empirical studies with glowing points and structures; many data points being united.

## What he rejected

- Motion studies 2 Gleichtakt, 3 Sternbild, 4 Schwarm: "lame", "oll". Not to be developed.
- The disc in frame 5 of the second sketch.
- The seven earlier storyboards (`index.html`, `concepts/`): nice, but none shows the sharing of data. The animated Hands intro is a stopgap.
- **SVG as the way to make it. "SVG, damit bin ich durch."** No more agents sent off to draw this in SVG. (The motion studies are programmed canvas, not SVG; he has not judged that method separately.)

## Not Big Brother

Stars sending their data to a middle is fine. Big Brother would be *people* shown connected by lines and sharing data.

## The story, in his words

Millions of people with this illness collect data points every day, each alone; there are hardly any studies because the illness is underfunded. Here the data is collected for free, unified and brought together for a crossover evaluation. If something helps several people, the signal shows; the statistics are published, so others find it too. What the picture story should carry: science, sharing data, bringing enlightenment together, detecting signals together and letting the group learn, a distributed system, cooperation, integration, the statistical empirical process. Take a part of that and make a simple, beautiful picture story with glowing points. At the end light, or hands in a circle, or only points. It does not have to be about people.

Spoken lines used in the sketches: "Every day, millions of us notice what helps." / "Alone, it's a guess." / "Together, it's a signal." / "MEjority."

## Open: which structures

His question: in which algebraic, geometric and statistical structures could the stars arrange themselves so that a good story comes out?

Ideas from Claude, not yet seen or judged by Alex:
- **Scatter becomes a line:** loose points, then a trend runs through them (frame 4 of his favourite sketch).
- **Two heaps, one shifted:** two bell curves of points (the study he liked).
- **Pairs:** each person's placebo point and real point joined by a short line; most lines tilt the same way. This is the crossover itself.
- **The band tightens:** a wide glowing band of uncertainty narrows as more points arrive.
- **The wandering point settles:** an average drifts about with few points and comes to rest with many.
- **Snap to a grid:** scattered points fall into one common grid; the picture of unifying data.
